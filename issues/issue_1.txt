Server Crash mid Trade 
**Setup**

- Alice has a resting SELL order: 100 BTC at ₹50,000, sitting in the book, `reserved_btc = 100` in the DB, order status `pending`, `order_id = 501`.
- Bob sends a BUY order for 60 BTC at ₹50,000.

**What happens, step by step**

1. Bob's order hits `api.rs` → the handler calls `OrderBook::engine()`. This is pure in-memory. Inside `orderbook_buy()`:
   - `qty = min(60, 100) = 60`
   - Alice's resting order (`self.asks[i]`) has `qty -= 60` → now **40**, still not zero, so it stays `pending` in memory, no `fulfilled_ids` entry.
   - Bob's `match_data.qty -= 60` → 0, fully filled, no leftover.
   - A `Trade { buyer: Bob, seller: Alice, qty: 60, price: 50000 }` is pushed into `trades.trades`.
   - **At this exact moment**, the in-memory book already reflects "Alice sold 60" — but nothing has touched Postgres yet.

2. `engine()` returns. The handler now calls `settle_trades()` with that trade list.

3. Inside `settle_trades()`:
   - It fetches Alice's and Bob's balance rows from the DB (unchanged so far).
   - It computes new balances in memory: Bob `+60 BTC`, Alice `+₹30,00,000`, Alice's `reserved_btc -= 60`.
   - **First DB write**: `INSERT INTO trades (buyer_id, seller_id, qty, price) VALUES (Bob, Alice, 60, 50000)`. This succeeds and commits immediately — it's its own standalone statement, no transaction wrapping it with what comes next.

4. **Right here, the server crashes.** Could be an actual panic, an out-of-memory kill, a `kubectl` rolling deploy restart, a `sqlx` connection pool timeout on the *next* query, the machine losing power — doesn't need to be exotic. It just needs to happen between step 3's `INSERT INTO trades` and the `UPDATE balances` call a few lines later.

**State of the world after the crash**

- `trades` table: says Bob bought 60 BTC from Alice at ₹50,000. This trade is now permanent, auditable history.
- `balances` table: Alice's `reserved_btc` is still 100 (never updated). Bob's `balance_btc` is still unchanged (never got his 60 BTC). Alice never got her ₹30,00,000.
- `orders` table: Alice's order 501 still says `qty=100, status='pending'` — the partial-fill `qty=40` update never made it to disk (as I noted, partial fills aren't even persisted in this code, only full fulfillment is).
- In-memory orderbook: gone. Server restarts, `load_from_db()` reloads Alice's order fresh from Postgres as `qty=100, pending`.

**The aftermath**

The system now believes, simultaneously:
- "A trade of 60 BTC happened between Bob and Alice" (per `trades`)
- "Alice still has all 100 BTC reserved and available to sell" (per `balances` + reloaded orderbook)
- "Bob never received any BTC" (per `balances`)

Alice's order goes back into the live book at full size. It can now match against a *second* buyer, say Charlie, for another 60 BTC — and that trade will settle "normally," debiting Alice's `reserved_btc` by 60, leaving her at 40 reserved even though she only ever had 100 total and has now been recorded as selling 120.

So the money didn't vanish in the sense of disappearing from the universe — it's just that the ledger now has one buyer (Bob) who paid-and-got-nothing recorded in the trade log but not reflected anywhere in his balance, one seller who's been sold out from under twice, and no way to tell from the `balances` table alone that anything is wrong. You'd only catch it by cross-checking `trades` against `balances`, which nothing in this codebase currently does.

That's the story a transaction wrapping fund-lock + trade insert + balance update + order status + resting-order persistence into one atomic commit is meant to prevent: either *all* of that happens, or *none* of it does, and a crash mid-way just means the whole settlement gets retried or discarded cleanly instead of leaving a half-written trade behind.
